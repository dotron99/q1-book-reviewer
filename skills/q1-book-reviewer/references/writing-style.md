# Writing and template matching

Use the requested journal and reference as the design authority. The included preset is a neutral fallback, not an IJHS, MDPI or Elsevier official template.

## Read the accepted reference on three levels

- **Structure:** bibliographic opening; overall assessment; scope and method; selected examples; sustained critique; readership and contribution; references and declarations as required.
- **Voice:** strength of judgement, balance between exposition and criticism, terminology, spelling, quotation density and use of the reviewer's perspective.
- **Presentation:** page geometry, font, paragraph spacing/indentation, bibliographic emphasis, references, byline and footer. Inspect the rendered reference rather than relying only on XML.

Preserve useful design conventions. Do not duplicate distinctive sentences, objections or a conclusion from the old review. A previous review's outside bibliography is evidence about its scholarly approach, not a set of citations to reuse without checking relevance and source passages.

## Drafting decisions

A short review needs one coherent evaluative argument more than exhaustive chapter coverage. Organise examples by the problem they illuminate when that serves the assessment. Use concrete objects, methods or cases instead of generic praise. Give an alternative reading enough space to make the criticism fair. Avoid treating the author's stated scope as a failure unless the wider claim actually exceeds it.

Use plain academic prose and precise verbs. Avoid routine phrases such as 'timely and important', 'a must-read', 'groundbreaking' and claims that no prior work exists without evidence. Do not insert a fixed quota of praise, criticism, paragraphs or citations. The preset's twelve body slots are a drafting aid and may be replaced by the structure that suits the brief.

Page numbers refer to the cited edition's printed pagination unless the journal specifies otherwise. Do not add quotation marks to a paraphrase. Do not quote extracted text without checking character errors, omissions and context against the source page.

## Draft markers

Use `[[TODO: author decision]]` and `[[VERIFY: source claim]]`. Make the entire marker red in Word. These are working indicators, never publishable text. Once resolved, replace the marker with confirmed text in black. Do not turn a missing fact into a confident claim merely to remove a marker.
